How to Use Yardi Analytics to Make
Better Portfolio Decisions

Voyager 8, Yardi Data Connect, and Virtuoso have expanded what analytics is possible inside Yardi. Most organizations are using a fraction of it. Here is what to do differently.

Artisan Solutions 7 min read Yardi · Analytics · Reporting

Most organizations running Yardi have more analytical capability than they realize. The standard reports are there: rent rolls, aging schedules, occupancy snapshots, general ledger summaries. They run reliably and answer the questions that Yardi was configured to answer out of the box. The issue is that these are rarely the questions your leadership team is actually asking in 2026.

Property portfolios have grown more complex. Investors want multi-entity consolidated views. Asset managers want delinquency trends and lease expiry profiles weeks before month-end, not after it. Finance teams want budget-versus-actual visibility that does not require an overnight spreadsheet exercise. Standard reports, however well-structured, were not designed to answer these questions at the speed and depth modern portfolios require.

Voyager 8 and the products built around it have significantly expanded what is possible. The question is not whether the capability exists. It is whether your organization has set it up and is actually using it.

01
The foundation

What Yardi's analytics layer actually covers

Yardi's analytics capabilities span several distinct tools, and understanding which does what is essential before deciding what to configure. The core reporting engine inside Voyager has always been comprehensive, but Voyager 8 introduced a set of purpose-built operational dashboards that address the most common reporting bottlenecks directly.

The Collections Dashboard gives finance teams real-time visibility into resident and tenant payment activity: who owes, how much, and for how long. It replaces the spreadsheet that most organizations built alongside Yardi to track delinquency. The Renewals Dashboard brings lease expiry and renewal status into one view, replacing manual tracking across multiple reports. The Period Close Dashboard tracks month-end progress across tasks and properties. The Tenant Risk Dashboard surfaces commercial tenants whose financial position warrants attention.

Sitting above these is Yardi Spreadsheet Reporting, known as YSR, which connects Voyager data directly to Excel and Word templates using mapped data fields. This is the platform Yardi has positioned as the primary reporting format for Voyager 8: new features and new analytics capabilities are built for YSR first. For finance teams already working in Excel, this creates a powerful bridge: formatted financial packages, board reports, and property-level summaries that draw live data from Voyager without any manual export step.

Voyager 8 also introduced Report Builder, a drag-and-drop tool for building operational reports without requiring technical expertise. For organizations that have previously relied entirely on Yardi's standard report library or custom SQL work, Report Builder opens a meaningful middle ground where operational teams can create and maintain their own views without developer involvement.

JLL research found that only 13% of real estate companies have access to real-time business intelligence. The tools to change that are already inside Yardi for most organizations. The gap is configuration, not capability.

02
Executive-level visibility

Yardi Data Connect and Power BI

For organizations that want portfolio-level analytics in a modern business intelligence environment, Yardi Data Connect is the route to take. Data Connect creates a governed, secure data pipeline from Voyager through Microsoft Azure into Power BI. It eliminates manual exports, honors Voyager's property-level security settings, and adds row-level security inside Power BI so the right people see only what they should.

The product ships with a starter pack: hundreds of prebuilt data points, dashboards, and visualizations covering the metrics that asset managers and executives typically need first. Box score, pending occupancy, scheduled charges, rent roll, budget versus actual, delinquency and accounts receivable aging, leasing funnel, general ledger transactions, work orders, vendor spend. These are configurable, and custom metrics and visualizations can be built on top of them as the organization's needs evolve.

The result, when configured properly, is a set of live Power BI dashboards that refresh automatically from Voyager data. Leadership can open a dashboard on any device and see an accurate, current view of the portfolio without waiting for a report to be run, formatted, and emailed. For portfolios spanning multiple entities, multiple geographies, or both residential and commercial assets, this kind of consolidated live view is where Data Connect earns its value quickly.

The setup requires planning. Data Connect works best when the underlying Voyager data is clean and consistently structured across properties. Organizations that have grown through acquisition and have inconsistent property codes, charge codes, or accounting configurations will need to address those issues before the Power BI output becomes reliable. This is where specialist configuration work pays dividends, often significantly faster than attempting it internally.

03
Natural language and AI

What Yardi Virtuoso adds to analytics

Yardi Virtuoso, launched at the Yardi Advanced Solutions Conference in September 2025, introduced a capability that changes how less technical users can interact with portfolio data. Among its features is natural language querying: the ability to ask Voyager a question in plain language and receive a data-driven response, directly inside the Voyager 8 interface.

For property managers and asset managers who know what they need to know but have not always had an easy route to the underlying data, this is genuinely useful. A leasing manager can ask a question about a property's current availability without running a specific report. A regional manager can ask for a summary of delinquency across their portfolio without building a query. The Virtuoso Support agent, already live in Voyager 8 and CRM IQ with hundreds of clients active, handles these kinds of conversational interactions alongside workflow guidance.

It is worth being precise about what natural language querying does and does not replace. It is a fast access layer for common questions, not a substitute for structured reporting or board-level analytics. The dashboards, YSR-powered financial packages, and Data Connect-driven Power BI outputs remain the right tools for anything that needs to be formatted, auditable, or shared outside Voyager. Virtuoso makes day-to-day data access faster and more accessible. The two capabilities complement each other.

  • Voyager 8 purpose-built dashboards cover collections, renewals, period close, and tenant risk
  • Yardi Spreadsheet Reporting connects Voyager data live to Excel and Word templates
  • Report Builder enables drag-and-drop operational reporting without developer support
  • Yardi Data Connect creates a secure automated pipeline from Voyager to Power BI via Azure
  • Virtuoso adds natural language data querying directly inside Voyager 8
  • Clean, consistent Voyager data is the prerequisite for reliable analytics output at every layer
04
Where organizations fall short

The configuration gap that limits most analytics

The most common reason Yardi analytics underperforms is not the tool. It is the data behind it. Property codes that are not consistently applied. Charge codes that differ between properties acquired at different times. Accounting periods that do not align cleanly across entities. Unit categorizations that made sense locally but do not aggregate correctly at portfolio level.

These inconsistencies are invisible in day-to-day operations because each property runs correctly in isolation. They surface immediately when you try to aggregate data across the portfolio, which is precisely what analytics demands. A delinquency dashboard that shows 12 of 14 properties is less useful than one that shows none, and it is worse than no dashboard at all if the missing two are the ones with the greatest risk.

Getting analytics to work well across a portfolio typically requires a structured data review: mapping how properties are coded, how charges are categorized, and how the chart of accounts aligns across entities. In organizations that have grown through acquisition this work can be substantial, but it is finite. Once done, the analytics layer performs consistently and the ongoing maintenance is minimal. The organizations that invest in this groundwork are the ones whose leadership teams stop asking for the report to be emailed and start opening the dashboard directly.

YSR configuration deserves the same attention. Mapping Voyager data fields to Excel templates correctly, and building a standardized template library that covers the reports leadership actually needs, is a one-time setup exercise with long-running returns. Teams that have done this properly run their entire board reporting pack from Voyager without a single manual export or formula check.

Analytics readiness · What to assess before configuring Foundation first

What strong Yardi analytics requires

Consistent property codes and charge codes applied uniformly across all entities
A chart of accounts that aggregates cleanly at portfolio level, not just property level
Clearly defined accounting periods aligned across multi-entity structures
A reporting template library mapped for YSR, covering financial packages and board reports
Clear ownership of which team configures and maintains each analytics layer
An agreed refresh cadence for Data Connect if Power BI dashboards are in scope
05
Putting it to work

Building an analytics approach that actually gets used

Analytics tools fail for a practical reason: they are configured for the system, not the user. A beautiful Power BI dashboard that requires IT to update its connection settings each quarter will not get used. A YSR template that produces a report the finance team immediately reformats will not save time. The configuration work only pays off when the output is something real users open instead of building their own version in a spreadsheet.

The most effective approach starts with the questions leadership actually asks before month-end closes: the calls to the finance team, the emails requesting occupancy snapshots, the requests for delinquency summaries across specific sub-portfolios. These are the use cases to configure first. When those questions can be answered by opening a dashboard or running a single report, trust in the analytics layer grows quickly. From that foundation, the more sophisticated capability, cross-portfolio consolidated views, budget-versus-actual at asset level, lease expiry profiles across the commercial book, is something users will actively seek out rather than ignore.

A phased approach works well. Start with the Voyager 8 operational dashboards: Collections, Renewals, Period Close. These require no external tooling and deliver immediate value to the teams that live in Voyager daily. Add YSR templates for the three or four reports that currently consume the most manual effort. Then, once the data foundation is clean and the teams are confident in Voyager as a source of truth, layer in Data Connect for executive-level Power BI visibility. Virtuoso comes at no additional infrastructure cost for Voyager 8 users and can be used throughout, accelerating day-to-day access without displacing any of the structured reporting.

—
Closing thought

The data is already in Yardi

Every transaction, every lease event, every charge, every payment: it is already in Voyager. The data required to answer almost any question leadership can ask about a portfolio is sitting in the system. What most organizations are missing is not the information. It is the configuration that surfaces it cleanly, in the right format, for the right people, without a manual step in the middle.

Voyager 8, Data Connect, and Virtuoso represent a significant expansion of what Yardi makes possible on the analytics front. The organizations that take advantage of this are not necessarily the largest ones or the most technically sophisticated. They are the ones that have invested in getting the data foundation right and then configured the reporting layer to serve the people who need it.

If your leadership team is still waiting for someone to email them the numbers, that is the problem worth solving this quarter.
